## 1. Problem

Most catalogue content ships with no audio description track. A blind or low-vision viewer gets the dialogue and the sound design, and nothing about what the camera is showing: who entered the room, what the character is holding, where the scene moved to. Where description does exist, it was produced by hand, per title, which is why coverage is thin and uneven.

The workarounds available today all live off the television. A phone-synced companion (ActiView and similar) asks the viewer to hold a second device next to the screen and keep it aligned. A pre-mixed descriptive audio track, when a distributor produces one, is a file: no toggle, no pacing control, no way to skip a cue.

This hackathon is the occasion, and it constrains the work (`limits.hackathon`):

- submission deadline **2026-10-23 12:00 PT** — 34 days from this draft
- the repository must be **either public with a detectable open-source license file, or private and shared with `testing@devpost.com` and six named Amazon Developer Relations GitHub accounts** — and either way must hold all source, assets and clear setup/run instructions (`limits.hackathon.repo_must_be`)
- the demo video is at most **3 minutes**, in **english**
- judging is two stages: a pass/fail viability stage, then **five equally weighted criteria** — tech implementation, design, potential impact, quality of idea, and a friction-log bonus of up to **10%**

The documents in this set are written in English (`decisions.docs_in_english`) because the judges read the repository either way, and submission materials are required to be in English regardless. Note that `decisions.docs_in_english` was originally justified by the repository being *necessarily* public; re-reading the rules in round R10 showed private-and-shared is an equal option. The decision stands on its second reason, which is the one that was always load-bearing.

## 2. Chosen solution

An app on **Vega OS** that plays a video and speaks generated descriptions in the silences.

The pipeline runs offline, before playback: a first pass builds a *dramatis personae* cast anchor from opening-scene frames + subtitle speaker cues (`decisions.dramatis_personae`, one Bedrock call), then per gap it reads the asset's subtitles, finds every dialogue gap of at least **1500 ms** (`limits.ad.min_gap_ms`), extracts up to **3** frames per gap (`limits.ad.frames_per_gap_max` — the midpoint, plus per-shot frames when the gap spans a cut), sends them with the surrounding subtitle context, the cast anchor, and the preceding **10** descriptions (`limits.ad.rolling_context_cues`, so names stay consistent across the film) to a vision model on Bedrock, and bounds the returned description to what fits in the gap at **160 wpm** (`limits.ad.speaking_rate_wpm`) rescaled by the selected verbosity (`decisions.verbosity_levels`, `limits.ad.verbosity_scales`). Polly synthesises each cue. The output is a description track — one file per verbosity level, matching `contracts.description_track` and declaring its own level — plus its audio clips.

**Deferred alongside it** (`decisions.typesafe_judgment_layer`): a TypeSafe text-only judgment layer, `C15` normalizing each cue's character reference against the cast list and `C16` screening subtitle lines for injection intent. Both are `kind: deferred` as of review R5 — the problems they address already carry mitigations, a third non-Amazon vendor adds a key and a setup step to a repo the judges must run, and `C15` rewrites cue text *after* the word budget is applied, which can push a bounded cue past its gap. They reopen if Phase 1 closes green before 09-30.

On the device, the app loads that track, schedules each cue against playback position, ducks the main audio to **25%** (`limits.ad.duck_target_pct`) over **200 ms** (`limits.ad.duck_ramp_ms`), plays the clip, and restores. The remote toggles description on and off without interrupting playback and switches verbosity (`concise / standard / detailed`) between tracks (`C5`, `AC17`). The submission video visualizes the gap mechanism itself — a timeline strip with dialogue blocks vs gaps and a split-screen before/after (silence vs ducking + description) rendered from `C7` data (`decisions.gap_visualization`, `AC19`).

**A gap is not a cue, and the real film proved it.** Running `C7` against *Tears of Steel* found 38 gaps — but five of them run longer than 30 seconds and together carry 68% of all the words, the longest being 167 seconds with a 444-word ceiling. One description covering 167 seconds is not a description, and three frames cannot represent a minute and a half of film. So a gap longer than `limits.ad.max_cue_ms` (**12 s**) is split into consecutive cue windows, each with its own frames and its own budget, and a window that cannot carry `limits.ad.min_useful_words` (**3**) is dropped rather than filled with noise — 13 of the 38 gaps are 1.5–2 s and yield one or two words at concise. Thirty-eight gaps become about seventy cues.

**Why this shape.** A description that runs past its gap talks over dialogue, which is worse than no description — so the word budget is derived from the gap, not from the model. `limits.ad.max_words_per_cue` is that ceiling and it is physical: no verbosity level may exceed it. The three levels are therefore *targets under* it (`limits.ad.verbosity_scales`, ×0.6 / ×0.85 / ×1.0), not multipliers of it — `detailed` spends the whole gap, and nothing has to overrun for the levels to differ. And the gap structure is already in the subtitle file, which means the hard timing problem is solved by data that ships with the asset rather than by inference.

**Two paths, one of them guaranteed** (`decisions.hybrid_ad_path`). The prepared track above is the base path and depends on nothing unverified. Live frame capture at runtime — the same model call, but on the frame the viewer is watching — is an **optional layer** (`C11`, `kind: deferred`) that ships only if `D1` is confirmed in Phase 0. The demo never depends on it.

**Product name** (`decisions.product_name`): *Interstice*, `com.cesarrivasp.interstice`. Chosen after a name search killed the alternatives — AudioLens is taken commercially twice, Narra is taken by an AI video-narration app in this exact space, and EchoScene collides with an ECCV 2024 paper and repository of the same name in the same field.

**Hackathon entry** (`decisions.hackathon_track`): Fire TV as the primary track, on Vega OS, with both mini challenges on top — AWS Builder through Bedrock and Polly, Open Source through the extracted package (`decisions.oss_package_scope`). A submission may win at most one track prize and one mini-challenge prize.

**Why not the alternatives.** `A1` second-screen companion: requires a phone held next to the TV, and the thesis here is that the television does it alone. `A2` burned-in descriptive audio: a file, with no runtime control. `A3` subtitle text-to-speech with no vision: reads aloud dialogue that is already audible and says nothing about the screen — it fails the premise and the track's computer-vision priority at once. `A4` a Fire OS Android build: accepted by the rules, but it is the crowded half of the track; it reopens immediately if `D3` dies.

## 4. Data specification

The description track is the interface between the pipeline and the app, and the only contract in the set. It matches `contracts.description_track`:

```json
{
  "version": "string",
  "asset_id": "string",
  "generated_at": "string",
  "source_subtitles": "string",
  "verbosity": "concise | standard | detailed",
  "model_id": "string",
  "cues": "array<description_cue>"
}
```

Each cue matches `contracts.description_cue`:

```json
{
  "id": "string",
  "start_ms": "int",
  "end_ms": "int",
  "words": "int",
  "text": "string",
  "audio_uri": "string",
  "source_frames_ms": "array<int>",
  "status": "ok | failed"
}
```

`status: failed` exists so a throttled or rejected cue does not break the track: the pipeline emits the cue with no text and the app skips it. A track that fails to produce one cue still plays. `source_frames_ms` is a list because a gap spanning a cut is described from up to `limits.ad.frames_per_gap_max` frames, and the track has to record which ones — otherwise a cue cannot be audited against the picture afterwards. `verbosity` is what lets `C6` know which of the three generated tracks it loaded; the files are named `<asset_id>.<verbosity>.track.json` beside the asset and a level switch resolves by that name, falling back to `standard` (`decisions.verbosity_levels`).

Endpoints (`endpoints`): `bedrock-runtime.{AWS_REGION}.amazonaws.com InvokeModel` and `polly.{AWS_REGION}.amazonaws.com SynthesizeSpeech`. A third is registered but **not called while `C15`/`C16` are deferred** — `api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone POST (Authorization: Bearer TYPESAFE_API_KEY)`; it stays in the registry so the entries reopen with a verified endpoint rather than a remembered one. Configuration is by environment variable, names only, never values (`env_vars`): `AWS_REGION`, `BEDROCK_MODEL_ID`, `POLLY_VOICE_ID`, `DEMO_ASSET_PATH`, `DEMO_SUBTITLES_PATH`, `TYPESAFE_API_KEY`.

## 5. Security

**The live layer sends screen content to a third party.** `C11`, if it ships, transmits a frame of what the viewer is watching to Bedrock. It is off by default, requires an explicit in-app opt-in that names the destination before the first send, and the README states it (`decisions.privacy_optin`, `AC9`). The prepared path sends nothing at runtime — the model calls all happen offline, on the developer's machine, against an asset that is already public.

**Subtitle text is untrusted input to a model.** `C9` feeds subtitle lines into the prompt as context. A subtitle file is third-party content and can carry text that reads as instructions. Mitigation: subtitles are passed as delimited data, the response is bounded to `limits.ad.max_words_per_cue`, and a response that does not match the expected shape is rejected and the cue emitted as `status: failed`. A second, earlier layer was designed and then deferred (`C16`, `decisions.typesafe_judgment_layer`): a TypeSafe Noul call screening each subtitle line before it reaches the prompt. While it is deferred the delimited-data and shape-validation mitigation above is the whole defence, which is acceptable here because the subtitle file is one the author chooses and controls (`decisions.demo_asset_licensing`) rather than arbitrary user input.

**TypeSafe judgments are text-only** — recorded so a later round does not re-propose what review R4 already ruled out. `C15` and `C16` (`decisions.typesafe_judgment_layer`, both deferred) run on Jev/System One, which accepts no image or video input. Neither call ever sees a frame, and neither could substitute for `AC14`'s human watch of the track against the picture.

**Content rights** (`decisions.demo_asset_licensing`). The demo plays an openly licensed asset with published subtitles — a Blender Foundation open movie (CC-BY) is the default candidate — and the asset must carry **substantial dialogue**: the gap structure the whole mechanism rests on comes from the subtitle file, so a near-dialogue-free film (Big Buck Bunny, Sintel) collapses the demo into one continuous gap. The candidates are *Tears of Steel* and *Elephants Dream*, and subtitle availability and quality are verified in Phase 0, before any pipeline work. The rules require the submission video to carry no unauthorized third-party copyrighted material, and that video is published publicly on YouTube. No commercial or personally recorded content appears anywhere in the submission.

**Credentials.** AWS credentials stay in the environment of the offline pipeline. Nothing in the app bundle holds a key: the device reads a generated track file, it does not call AWS. If `C11` ships, that assumption changes and the layer needs its own answer before it is written — which is one more reason it is deferred.

## 6. Cost and scalability

This is a demo with one asset and one viewer, and it is worth stating plainly: none of the work here exists to answer a limit of scale. Volume is a single film, processed once, offline.

Cost is bounded by the number of **cues**, not gaps: one Bedrock call (carrying up to `limits.ad.frames_per_gap_max` frames) and one Polly call per cue, run once per regeneration. `worst_case` now holds measured figures for the gap half — **39 gaps, 60 cues after splitting, 181 Bedrock calls** (60 × 3 verbosity levels + the dramatis personae pass). The figure has moved twice, each time on a measurement: ~121 assumed one cue per gap and was low by 84%; 211 counted the credits as describable; 181 is the track after `clipToContent` removes them. `est_cost_usd` stays `null` because `D4` means no call has ever been priced. The `$150` of promotional AWS credits (`limits.hackathon.aws_credits_usd`) is requested in Phase 0 and is expected to cover the entire build. With `C15`/`C16` deferred there is no second vendor on the bill.

Two numbers are unknown by design rather than by omission. `limits.bedrock.invoke_rate_per_account` is `unknown`: the pipeline is offline and batched, so a throttle costs wall-clock time and never a broken demo — the response is exponential backoff, at most 5 attempts, then the cue is emitted as `status: failed` and the track still ships. `limits.polly.chars_per_request` is `unknown` for the same reason: a cue is bounded to `limits.ad.max_words_per_cue`, roughly two orders of magnitude below any documented Polly bound, so it is not expected to bind. Both are confirmed against the console on the first run, not guessed now.

The one real memory constraint is on the device, and it is not about scale either: a Fire TV Stick is a 32-bit process with a small heap, so clips stream from disk and at most **8** are resident (`limits.clip_cache.max_clips_in_memory`) — verified on the worst device in the matrix, not on the simulator.
